# The desk map goes

The desk map — the composed outline of every note, agent, page and
conversation, drawn into one editor you could type into — is gone. So is
the overview it lived behind.

## What stops working

`o` no longer opens anything, and neither does `e`, which showed the map's
raw source. The map's own keys go with it: the heading verbs (new sibling,
new child, promote, demote, move subtree up and down), the fold cycle, the
row cursor, rename-topic, and the map's search. `space r` and the rail that
came with the overview are gone; leaving the browser lands on Home instead.

The preview pane goes with the overview. A page — a browser tab you had
filed, or one ctrl-clicked out of another — used to draw beside the map, so
you could look at it without leaving the outline. There is no beside any
more: a page is a surface like every other, and looking at one means
opening it.

## What to do instead

- **To reach a thing** — the finder. It offers every node as a path:
  `nixos › poco on linux › warm agent`, the page under a topic, an agent by
  any name it answers to, and a thing under each label it carries as well
  as where it sits. Typing part of any of those reaches it, and choosing it
  opens it.
- **To act on a thing** — the card. A verdict, a label, a file-under is
  about the surface in front of you, or the card under Home's cursor when
  Home is what you are reading. That was already true; the map was the
  third answer, and it was the one that made "what is this about?" hard to
  predict.
- **To write a note** — `n n`. The note opens as its own surface, ready to
  type, and files under wherever the cursor was. On an empty desk it makes
  the first note at the root.
- **To read a note** — open it from the finder, from Home, or from a card.
  It is its own editor over its own body, rather than a slice of one big
  one.
- **To look at a page** — open it, from the finder or from its card, and it
  takes the frame. A tab ctrl-clicked out of another is still filed under
  the page it came from, so it is where you would look for it; it just no
  longer appears on its own beside the map.

## What is lost with no replacement

Staffing a topic directly — `r` on an unstaffed heading, which opened a
draft with that topic already attached — has no route left. You can still
make an agent with `n a` and file it, but the one keystroke that said "put
someone on this" was a map row's verb and went with the map.

## Why

The map had to be composed before it could be read, and composing it was
the cost of every desk event: one agent's news rebuilt an outline of the
whole desk. Everything the map was actually used for — finding a thing,
naming what a verb is about, reading a note — is answered from the store
directly and costs one row's work, which is what the finder cut established
and what this removes the alternative to.
